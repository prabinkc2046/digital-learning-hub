🎬 How I Create Professional Video Guides

My step-by-step workflow for creating short, clear, teacher-friendly how-to videos, from screen recording through to publishing on SharePoint and Compass.

Screen Recording AI Narration Video Editing Publishing

📖 About This Workflow

This guide documents the process I follow to create technology training videos and how-to guides for teachers.

My approach starts with recording a detailed demonstration in my own voice. I then convert the recording into a concise narration, refine the visuals, add highlights, and publish the finished resource where teachers can easily access it.

My guiding principle: Keep each video clear, purposeful and easy to follow. Give viewers enough time to see the action, hear the instruction and understand what is happening.

🧰 Tools I Use

Each tool has a specific purpose in my workflow.

🖥️ macOS Screen Recording Capture the screen and my original voice commentary.
🎞️ Microsoft Clipchamp Transcription, narration, timeline editing and music.
🤖 Microsoft Copilot Convert transcripts into concise instructional scripts.
🎨 Canva Video highlights, animations, privacy blurring and banners.
📁 SharePoint Store source files, completed videos and sharing links.
🏫 Compass Publish learning resources as tiles on the Digital Learning site.
📝 TextEdit Open SRT subtitle files and copy the transcript.
🔊 Text to Speech Generate polished narration and align it with the demonstration.

My production process follows this sequence.

🎥 Phase 1 — Recording and Preparing Content

01 Prepare and Screen Record the Demonstration Mac screen recording with my own commentary +

I start by demonstrating the process on screen while explaining everything in my own voice. At this stage, I focus on completeness rather than making the video short.

  1. Open the application or tool I want to demonstrate.
  2. Prepare the screen so the important controls are visible.
  3. Press Command + Shift + 5 on my Mac.
  4. Choose the appropriate screen recording option.
  5. Under Options, select the microphone I want to use.
  6. Start recording and talk through each action.
  7. Explain the steps slowly and clearly.
  8. Once finished, stop the recording.
My approach: I deliberately take my time when recording. I can remove unnecessary sections later, but I want the original recording to capture the entire process and explanation.
Important: Before recording, close unrelated windows, hide notifications and avoid displaying student or staff personal information.
02 Upload Original Recording to SharePoint Store the video in the correct Digital Learning folder +
  1. Open the Digital Learning SharePoint site.
  2. Navigate to the appropriate resource category.
  3. Open the correct application or equipment folder.
  4. Upload the original screen recording.
  5. Check the upload has completed successfully.

For example, for a CommBox training resource:

Why I do this: Storing the recording in the correct location keeps resources organised and makes the video easier to find during editing.
03 Open the Recording in Clipchamp Load the video into the editing timeline +
  1. Find the uploaded video in SharePoint.
  2. Right-click or open the file's available actions.
  3. Select Edit in Clipchamp.
  4. Allow the video to finish loading into Clipchamp.
  5. Click the + button to add the video to the timeline.
  6. Confirm the complete video is visible in the timeline.
Tip: Large recordings can take some time to load. I make sure the video is ready before proceeding with transcription or editing.
04 Transcribe the Original Recording Convert my spoken explanation into text +
  1. Open the transcription or captions feature in Clipchamp.
  2. Generate a transcription of the recorded audio.
  3. Review the generated text for obvious mistakes.
  4. Download the transcript as an SRT file.
  5. Find the downloaded file on my Mac.
  6. Open the SRT file using TextEdit.
  7. Select and copy the entire transcript.
Example SRT structure:

1
00:00:01,000 --> 00:00:05,000
In this video, we will learn how to register an NFC card.
Purpose: The transcript becomes the foundation for writing a shorter, more polished narration.

🎙️ Phase 2 — Script and Narration

05 Create a Concise Script Using AI Transform the transcript into a clear how-to narration +
  1. Open Microsoft Copilot or another approved AI tool.
  2. Paste the copied SRT transcript.
  3. Ask AI to create a short introduction.
  4. Ask for clear step-by-step narration in a concise instructional style, similar to Tango.
  5. Review the script against the actual video.
  6. Correct any inaccurate instructions, missing actions or unclear wording.

Example prompt I can use:

You are creating a professional how-to video for teachers. Using the transcript below, write a short introduction and concise step-by-step narration. Follow the actual sequence of actions in the recording. Keep the language simple, direct and instructional, similar to a Tango-style guide. Use short sentences suitable for text-to-speech. Do not invent features, buttons or actions that are not supported by the transcript. Organise the narration into short, numbered segments so each segment can be matched with its corresponding action in the video. Transcript: [Paste SRT transcript here]
My goal: The original recording might contain long explanations. I use AI to make the final narration more focused, while preserving the essential instructions.
06 Generate and Align AI Narration Clipchamp text-to-speech and timeline alignment +

I arrange my workspace so I can see both the script and Clipchamp at the same time.

LEFT SIDE: AI-generated script
RIGHT SIDE: Clipchamp video editor
  1. Keep the prepared narration visible on the left.
  2. Open the Text to Speech feature in Clipchamp.
  3. Copy one short narration segment.
  4. Paste it into the text-to-speech input.
  5. Generate the narration and listen to it.
  6. Add the generated audio to the timeline.
  7. Position it to match the corresponding action on screen.
  8. Repeat for each narration segment.

After positioning each segment, I play the video to check whether the spoken instruction matches the visual action.

Important: Narration must not run ahead of the demonstration. I allow enough time for the voice to finish, and leave short gaps where the viewer needs time to understand the action.

✂️ Phase 3 — Editing and Visual Enhancement

07 Review and Export the Narrated Video Check audio timing before moving to Canva +
  1. Play through the entire Clipchamp timeline.
  2. Check that every narration segment matches the correct visual action.
  3. Adjust narration placement where required.
  4. Allow natural pauses between instructional steps.
  5. Remove or mute the original commentary if it is no longer needed.
  6. When satisfied, export the narrated video to my Mac.
Checkpoint: The video should already make sense to a viewer before I begin adding visual highlights.
08 Trim and Refine the Video in Canva Remove unnecessary footage without rushing the viewer +
  1. Open Canva and create or open a video editing project.
  2. Upload the video exported from Clipchamp.
  3. Place the video in the Canva editing timeline.
  4. Identify sections with unnecessary waiting, delays or mistakes.
  5. Trim or split those sections as needed.
  6. Keep sufficient time for narration to finish naturally.
  7. Leave short pauses so viewers can absorb the information.
  8. Watch the complete trimmed video before adding highlights.
My editing rule: Do not cut too aggressively. The purpose is to make the video clear and efficient, not simply as short as possible.
09 Add Animated Highlights and Check Privacy Use Canva visual elements to guide the viewer +

After trimming, I add visual cues to direct the viewer's attention to important controls and actions.

  1. Open Canva Elements.
  2. Add a rectangle shape.
  3. Set the fill to transparent or use an outline-only rectangle.
  4. Use a red border with rounded corners.
  5. Position it around the relevant button, menu or selected element.
  6. Apply a Wipe animation where appropriate.
  7. Adjust its appearance and timing to align with the action.
  8. Repeat for other important interactions.
  9. Review the entire video for private information.
  10. Blur, cover or remove any sensitive information that appears.
  11. Play through the video and export it as an MP4.
Visual style: I use red rounded rectangles and subtle wipe animations to draw attention to the interface elements being demonstrated.
Privacy check: Pay particular attention to student names, email addresses, personal records, passwords and any other confidential information. Check the video frame by frame where necessary.
10 Add Background Music in Clipchamp Final audio mix and completed video export +

I return to the original Clipchamp project. I keep that project open until the production process is finished.

  1. Return to the existing Clipchamp project.
  2. Clear the existing timeline after confirming the required exports are safely saved.
  3. Upload the enhanced MP4 exported from Canva.
  4. Add the video to the timeline.
  5. Open the audio or music library.
  6. Add background music, usually Keep Moving.
  7. Reduce the music volume to approximately 10%.
  8. Align the music so it starts with the video.
  9. If the music is too short, select it and press Command + D to duplicate it.
  10. Align the duplicated audio clips without unwanted gaps.
  11. Trim the music so it finishes with the video.
  12. Add fade-in or fade-out effects where appropriate.
  13. Listen to the complete video and export the final version.
My preferred music: Keep Moving, usually at 10% volume. The spoken instructions must always remain clearly understandable over the background music.
Additional check: Confirm the selected music is licensed for your intended educational use and distribution.

🚀 Phase 4 — Publishing and Distribution

11 Upload Final Video and Create a Banner Organise the finished resource in SharePoint +
  1. Return to the correct Digital Learning SharePoint folder.
  2. Upload the completed MP4 video.
  3. Give it a clear and descriptive title.
  4. Open Canva and find an existing video banner template.
  5. Create a copy of the existing banner.
  6. Replace the heading with the title of the new guide.
  7. Use Copilot to generate an illustrative image related to the topic.
  8. Replace the old banner image with the new one.
  9. Check that the heading and image are clearly visible.
  10. Download the finished banner as a PNG image.
Example video title:
How to Register an NFC Card on the CommBox
Consistency: Reusing the existing Canva banner template helps keep learning resources visually consistent across the Digital Learning site.
12 Share the Video and Publish on Compass Create the final Digital Learning tile +
  1. Find the completed video in SharePoint.
  2. Select the video and click Share.
  3. Open the link settings.
  4. Choose the appropriate permission allowing people within King's to view the resource.
  5. Set the link to view-only where available.
  6. Apply the sharing settings.
  7. Click Copy link.
  8. Open the Digital Learning site on Compass.
  9. Create a new tile in the appropriate section.
  10. Enter the title of the learning resource.
  11. Paste the copied SharePoint link in the Open URL field.
  12. Upload the Canva banner PNG as the tile image.
  13. Save or publish the tile.
  14. Open the tile and confirm it loads the correct video.
Final result: Staff can find the learning resource on Compass, recognise the topic from its banner and open the instructional video through SharePoint.
Final permission test: Check the link using the intended viewer's access level, not only your own account. This helps confirm staff can open the resource without needing additional permissions.

💡 Additional Recommendations

The workflow above reflects my current process. The following are optional improvements that could make future video production more efficient.

1. Define one learning objective

Before recording, identify exactly what the teacher should be able to do after watching. Where possible, focus each short video on one task.

2. Use a standard opening structure

Begin with the task, explain when it is useful, and then demonstrate the steps. Avoid lengthy introductions.

3. Standardise file naming

Use descriptive names and consistent folder structures for original recordings, working exports and final videos.

CommBox_Register_NFC_Card_Final.mp4
CommBox_Register_NFC_Card_Banner.png
4. Make resources accessible

Consider providing accurate captions, checking readability of on-screen text, and avoiding excessive animation. Retain a corrected transcript when useful.

5. Test the final video as a first-time viewer

Watch the video without relying on your own knowledge of the application. Check whether a teacher unfamiliar with the feature could follow each step.

6. Maintain a small resource register

Record the resource title, topic, intended audience, SharePoint link, publication date and last review date. This can help identify videos needing updates when software changes.

7. Keep editing projects and source files

Retain the original recording, editable projects, final narration and banner where practical. This makes it easier to update a guide when the software interface changes.
